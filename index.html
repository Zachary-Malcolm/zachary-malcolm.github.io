01 MSc dissertation

Prediabetes risk from DNA methylation

Spotting the confound that made the models look good.

I compared Logistic Regression, Random Forest, SVM and XGBoost for predicting prediabetes risk from blood DNA methylation at nearly 200,000 sites. While exploring the data I found that the chip version predicted the outcome perfectly. With that confound removed, the "promising" results disappeared.

  • Mean test ROC-AUC fell from 0.755 to 0.605 once the platform confound was removed, close to chance
  • Leakage-safe pipelines: scaling and LASSO + Random Forest feature selection refitted inside every cross-validation fold
  • Evaluated beyond accuracy: one model scored 71% accuracy while missing every low-risk person in the test set
  • Python
  • scikit-learn
  • XGBoost
  • pandas
  • LASSO
  • GEO data

03 MSc · Data Visualisation

What makes a hit? Acoustic features and song success

Do songs with certain combinations of sound do better in the charts?

Approach

  • Cleaned and merged Spotify audio features with Billboard Hot 100 chart history (MusicOSet)
  • Built a composite success score from standardised chart position, peak position and weeks on the chart
  • Reduced ten acoustic features with PCA, then grouped songs with k-means clustering
  • Designed every chart for accessibility: the colour-blind-safe viridis palette, minimal styling and readable labels

Findings

  • The first component measures intensity (energy 0.58, loudness 0.45); the second measures mood and rhythm (danceability 0.64, valence 0.44)
  • The most successful songs sit at the edges of the acoustic space, not in the crowded middle
  • One cluster, high in energy and loudness, had the highest median success and the most consistent results (but no profile guarantees a hit)
